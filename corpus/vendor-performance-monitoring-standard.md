# Vendor Performance Monitoring Standard

Doc ID: PERF-STD
Version: 1.2
Owner: Vendor Management Office
Effective: 2026-05-01
Status: Current

## Section 1 — Purpose

Due diligence tells us whether a vendor can deliver. Performance monitoring tells us whether it does. This standard sets how Vendor Managers track service levels, run business reviews, and act when performance slips.

## Section 2 — Review Cadence

| Tier | Business review | Scorecard |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | Quarterly business review (QBR) | Quarterly |
| Tier 2 | Semi-annual review | Semi-annual |
| Tier 3 | Annual review | Annual |
| Tier 4 | Not required | Not required |

QBRs are attended by the Vendor Manager, the Business Owner or delegate, and a vendor executive sponsor. Minutes and actions are recorded in ProcureNet within 5 business days of the meeting.

## Section 3 — Scorecards

3.1 Each scorecard rates the vendor Green, Amber, or Red on five dimensions:

- service levels: performance against the contractual SLAs;
- incident management: incidents notified and whether INCN-SLA deadlines were met;
- risk and compliance: status of open DUE-STD findings, exceptions, and overdue evidence;
- relationship: responsiveness, account management, and escalation handling;
- commercial: invoicing accuracy and adherence to agreed pricing.

3.2 The overall rating is the lowest rating among service levels, incident management, and risk and compliance. Relationship and commercial ratings are reported but do not set the overall rating.

3.3 A missed INCN-SLA notification deadline automatically sets incident management to Red for that period.

## Section 4 — KPIs

Contracts for Tier 1 and Tier 2 vendors must define measurable KPIs with target and threshold values. At minimum: service availability, incident response time, defect or error rate, and, for operations outsourcing, processing accuracy. KPIs are reported by the vendor monthly, with supporting data Northwind can verify.

## Section 5 — Underperformance

5.1 Any SLA breach is logged in ProcureNet with the service credit, if any, claimed.

5.2 A Red overall rating, or an SLA breach in two consecutive quarters, requires a performance improvement plan (PIP). The PIP sets specific actions, owners, and a remediation period of no more than 90 days.

5.3 If the PIP fails, the Vendor Manager escalates to the Business Owner and the TPRM Office, who decide whether to invoke contractual remedies or the exit plan (EXIT-PLN).

5.4 Service credits must be claimed. Waiving a service credit requires Business Owner approval recorded in ProcureNet.

## Section 6 — Inputs to Other Processes

The last four scorecards feed the renewal risk review (RNW-PRC Section 3). Red ratings for Tier 1 vendors are reported to the TPRC monthly. Scorecards and QBR minutes are retained under RET-SCH.

## Section 7 — Vendor Manager Responsibilities

The Vendor Manager owns the scorecard and may not delegate the rating to the vendor. Vendor-supplied data is an input, not the rating. Where the Vendor Manager changes, the outgoing manager hands over open PIPs, overdue evidence, and the current scorecard before leaving the role.

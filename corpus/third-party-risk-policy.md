# Third-Party Risk Management Policy

Doc ID: TPR-POL
Version: 4.0
Owner: Head of Third-Party Risk Management
Effective: 2026-01-15
Status: Current

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes how Northwind Capital Group identifies, assesses, contracts with, monitors, and exits third parties. Regulators hold Northwind accountable for activities performed on its behalf, so outsourcing a service never outsources the risk. This policy is the master document for Third-Party Risk Management (TPRM); the standards and procedures it references set the detailed requirements.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to every employee, contractor, and business unit that engages a third party. A third party is any external entity that provides goods or services to Northwind, receives Northwind data, connects to Northwind systems, or acts on Northwind's behalf with clients or regulators. This includes software-as-a-service providers, consultants, data brokers, outsourced operations, cloud service providers, and AI service providers. Intra-group affiliates are in scope where they provide services under an intercompany agreement.

Out of scope: utilities, government agencies acting in a regulatory capacity, and payments to individuals for employee reimbursements.

## 3. Roles and Responsibilities

- **Business Owner** — the senior employee accountable for the relationship. Initiates the engagement, owns the risk, and funds remediation.
- **Vendor Manager** — manages the relationship day to day, runs performance reviews (PERF-STD), and recertifies vendor access (ACC-STD).
- **TPRM Office** — assigns the risk tier (TIER-MTX), coordinates due diligence (DUE-STD), maintains the third-party inventory, and administers exceptions (EXC-PRC).
- **Procurement** — runs sourcing and competitive bidding (PRC-POL) and creates the vendor master record (ONB-PRC).
- **CISO office** — performs information security and cloud assessments (DUE-STD, CLD-STD).
- **Legal** — negotiates contract terms, including data processing (DPA-REQ) and audit rights (AUDT-GDE).
- **Financial Crimes Compliance (FCC)** — adjudicates sanctions and adverse media matches (SANC-PRC).

## 4. Lifecycle Requirements

### 4.1 Plan

Before approaching a vendor, the Business Owner documents the business need, the data the vendor will access, and the services it will support. The TPRM Office assigns a preliminary tier from this information within 5 business days of intake.

### 4.2 Due Diligence

Due diligence must be completed and approved before contract signature. The depth of diligence is set by tier under DUE-STD. Sanctions screening (SANC-PRC) and, for Tier 1 and Tier 2, financial review (FIN-STD) are part of diligence.

### 4.3 Contract

Contracts must include the clauses required for the vendor's tier: data protection, audit rights, incident notification (INCN-SLA), subcontractor controls (FRTH-POL), business continuity (BCP-REQ), and exit assistance (EXIT-PLN). No vendor may receive Northwind data, system access, or payment before onboarding is complete under ONB-PRC.

### 4.4 Monitor

Vendors are reassessed on the frequency set in TIER-MTX, and performance is monitored under PERF-STD. Concentration across the vendor portfolio is monitored under CONC-STD.

### 4.5 Exit

Tier 1 and Tier 2 relationships require a documented exit plan. Termination, data return, and access revocation follow EXIT-PLN.

## 5. Governance

The Third-Party Risk Committee (TPRC), chaired by the Chief Risk Officer, meets monthly. It receives the open exceptions register, the Tier 1 risk dashboard, and the quarterly concentration report. The Board Risk Committee receives an annual TPRM attestation. This policy is reviewed at least annually by the Head of Third-Party Risk Management.

## 6. Non-Compliance

Engaging a vendor outside this policy is a policy breach and is reported to the TPRC. Repeated or wilful breaches may result in disciplinary action. Where a requirement cannot be met, the Business Owner must request an exception under EXC-PRC before proceeding; retroactive exceptions are recorded as breaches.

# Concentration Risk Standard

Doc ID: CONC-STD
Version: 1.3
Owner: Head of Third-Party Risk Management
Effective: 2026-01-15
Status: Current

## 1. Purpose

Individually sound vendor relationships can add up to an unsound portfolio. When too many services depend on one vendor, one region, or one underlying technology, a single failure can disrupt several critical business services at once. This standard sets the thresholds Northwind monitors and what happens when one is crossed.

## 2. Types of Concentration

- **Vendor concentration** — dependence on a single vendor, including its affiliates, across many contracts.
- **Service concentration** — multiple critical business services relying on the same vendor.
- **Geographic concentration** — Tier 1 vendors delivering from the same country or metropolitan area, exposed to the same natural disaster, political, or infrastructure event.
- **Fourth-party concentration** — many vendors depending on the same subcontractor, most commonly the same cloud service provider (FRTH-POL, CLD-STD).
- **Substitutability** — services for which no alternative provider could be onboarded within 12 months.

## 3. Thresholds

The TPRM Office flags a concentration when any of these are met:

1. A single vendor group accounts for more than 15% of Northwind's total annual third-party spend.
2. A single vendor supports more than 3 critical business services.
3. More than 40% of Tier 1 vendors deliver primarily from the same country, excluding the United States.
4. A single fourth party supports more than 5 Tier 1 vendors.
5. A Tier 1 service is rated non-substitutable and has no tested exit plan under EXIT-PLN.

## 4. Assessment Points

Concentration is assessed:

- at onboarding of any Tier 1 vendor;
- at renewal of any Tier 1 vendor (RNW-PRC Section 3);
- when a Tier 1 vendor notifies a new material subcontractor;
- quarterly across the full portfolio.

## 5. Response to a Flag

A flag does not prohibit the engagement. It requires a decision. The Business Owner and the TPRM Office prepare a concentration memo stating the business rationale, the mitigations, and the residual risk. Mitigations may include a secondary provider, multi-region deployment, strengthened exit planning, or contractual step-in rights. The memo is approved by the TPRC. A threshold 2 or threshold 5 flag also requires Chief Risk Officer approval.

## 6. Reporting

The TPRM Office produces a quarterly concentration report for the TPRC covering all five thresholds, trends against the prior four quarters, and the status of approved mitigations. The report also lists the top 10 vendors by spend and the top 10 fourth parties by number of dependent Tier 1 vendors.

## 7. Data Sources

The report draws on the vendor inventory, the Business Impact Register, Accounts Payable spend data, and the fourth-party inventory maintained under FRTH-POL. Vendor Managers must keep the critical-service mapping in ProcureNet current; an inaccurate mapping is the most common cause of a missed concentration flag.

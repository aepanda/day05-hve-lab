# Right-to-Audit Clause Guide

Doc ID: AUDT-GDE
Version: 1.1
Owner: Legal — Commercial Contracts
Effective: 5 March 2026
Status: Current

## Section 1 — Why This Matters

Northwind's regulators expect us to be able to examine how our outsourced services are actually run, not just what the vendor says about them. A right-to-audit clause is how we keep that ability after signature. This guide explains what the clause must contain and which concessions negotiators can and cannot make.

## Section 2 — When the Clause Is Required

A right-to-audit clause is required in every Tier 1 and Tier 2 contract. It is recommended for Tier 3 contracts and is not required for Tier 4 (TIER-MTX §4).

## Section 3 — Required Elements

The clause must give Northwind, its internal audit function, its external auditors, and its regulators the right to:

- inspect premises, systems, records, and personnel involved in delivering the services;
- obtain copies of policies, procedures, and evidence of control operation;
- extend these rights to material subcontractors (FRTH-POL).

### 3.1 Notice

Routine audits may require up to 30 days' prior written notice. For-cause audits, triggered by a security incident, a regulatory request, or a suspected material breach of contract, may require no more than 10 business days' notice.

### 3.2 Frequency

The vendor may limit routine audits to once per 12-month period. For-cause audits and regulator audits may not be limited in number.

### 3.3 Regulator Access

Regulator access must be unconditional. The clause may not require regulators to give notice, sign confidentiality agreements with the vendor, or bear the vendor's costs. Do not accept any wording that makes regulator access subject to the vendor's consent.

### 3.4 Cost

Each party bears its own costs for routine audits. The vendor bears the cost of a for-cause audit that confirms a material breach.

## Section 4 — Negotiation Positions

Vendors with many clients often resist individual audits. Acceptable concessions:

- **Pooled audits.** Northwind may join a pooled audit with other clients in place of an individual routine audit.
- **Reliance on assurance reports.** A current SOC 2 Type II report meeting DUE-STD §4.2 may satisfy a routine audit for the controls in its scope. The clause must keep Northwind's right to audit controls outside the report's scope.
- **Remote audits.** Routine audits may be conducted remotely by default, with on-site access reserved for for-cause audits.

Concessions that are never acceptable: removing regulator access, removing for-cause audits, limiting audits to the vendor's own selection of records, or requiring Northwind to pay for the vendor's staff time on a routine audit beyond 3 days.

Any deviation from Section 3 not listed as an acceptable concession requires an exception under EXC-PRC.

## Section 5 — Exercising the Right

The Vendor Manager coordinates audit requests with Internal Audit and the TPRM Office. Audit findings are tracked as due diligence findings under DUE-STD §6.
